## I. The Structure of What Cannot Be Filed

The Compound Archive Overhang has a definition precise enough to audit and a structure invisible to nearly everyone who would need to act on it.

It grows in two simultaneous registers. In the temporal register: the gap between the outlier's earliest demonstrable capacity contribution — the analysis that called the result correctly before the result was in, the specific domain judgment no one else in the room made, the pre-evidential inference that the project's eventual success would confirm — and the organizational record's formal acknowledgment of it. In the credentialing register: the gap between the outlier's actual capacity, what a domain-competent source-blind evaluator would assess, and the institutionally documented version of that capacity circulating through the professional reference network. Both registers grow simultaneously. Both grow in the same direction. And they interact: the credentialing register's contamination inhibits the very reference conversations that could correct the temporal register's gap, while the temporal register's accumulated unacknowledged contributions provide the empirical content that each new credentialing conversation reproduces.

The structural property that makes the CAO the suppression architecture's most durable product is not its size. It is its invisibility from any single point within the institutional architecture that generated it.

An organizational actor who holds the current snapshot of the institutional record — the finalized performance reviews, the documented project attributions, the reference conversations that shaped the receiving organization's prior assessment — sees a set of documents that are internally consistent. The performance reviews cite team process. The project retrospectives attribute success to organizational methodology. The reference conversations reproduce the caricature with what appears to be independent multi-source confirmation. Nothing in the current snapshot contradicts anything else in the current snapshot. The record coheres.

The gap is not within the current snapshot. The gap is between the current snapshot and prior states of the record that the revision cycles eliminated. It is defined by the distance between temporal states — by the difference between what the institutional record contained at T₀ (when the contribution occurred and was witnessed) and what it contains at Tₙ (after each revision cycle extracted the threatening element and replaced it with the credentialed cover story). A document that reads as internally consistent from a snapshot position reads as a record of systematic suppression from a position that holds the full temporal sequence.

The outlier holds the full temporal sequence. They were present when the draft nearly acknowledged. They hold the email threads that document what the institutional record omitted. They know what both clauses said before the self-correcting denial ran its revision. The organizational actors who have the authority to initiate correction hold only the current snapshot, which reveals no gap to correct. The person who holds the complete record has no institutional authority to compel the comparison that would reveal the overhang.

This is the structure of the record that cannot be filed.

## II. The Temporal Register

The temporal register begins accumulating at the moment of the first contribution that the suppression architecture does not acknowledge.

Walsh and Ungson's (1991) foundational taxonomy of organizational memory identified five retention facilities through which institutional knowledge is stored and retrieved: individuals, culture, transformations (the routines through which information is encoded into organizational practice), structures (the formal administrative apparatus), and external archives. The outlier's contribution, from the first moment the suppression architecture operates, is systematically excluded from the retention facilities that generate organizational acknowledgment. Culture encodes the caricature rather than the contribution. Structures document the team process attribution rather than the specific judgment. The formal performance review records the caricature's predicted limitations rather than the actual output. The contribution survives in one retention facility only: the individual memory of the outlier and the documentary record the outlier maintains outside the institutional archive.

De Holan and Phillips (2004) distinguished active organizational forgetting — the motivated non-retrieval of information that would be costly to integrate — from passive memory decay. The temporal register's growth is the product of active forgetting in de Holan and Phillips's sense: each performance cycle in which the outlier's contribution is not retrieved from the organizational record is an inhibitory practice trial in the Anderson, Bjork, and Bjork (1994) retrieval-induced forgetting paradigm. The non-retrieval is not passive. It is the systematic output of an organizational memory architecture calibrated to the status-maintenance objective function.

The temporal register is therefore auditable in principle by any organizational actor who performs the comparison between the actual performance record — the project deliverables, the analytical artifacts, the email threads — and the formal institutional characterization of the person who produced them. The actual performance record is present in the organization's own systems. The comparison would reveal the gap. No one performs the comparison, and the Rational Inattention framework (Sims, 2003; Matějka & McKay, 2015; Maćkowiak, Matějka & Wiederholt, 2023) establishes precisely why this is the equilibrium rather than an oversight: the expected utility of finding the gap is negative under the status-maintenance objective function, because finding it would require updating the competence hierarchy at a social cost that exceeds the RI threshold for any actor operating that objective function. The RI-optimal strategy is not to perform the comparison — not through conscious avoidance but through the ordinary mechanism by which actions with negative expected utility fail to cross the attention threshold.

The temporal register therefore grows indefinitely, in direct proportion to the time elapsed since the first unacknowledged contribution, compounded by the number of subsequent contributions that the same architecture fails to acknowledge. It grows without anyone deciding to grow it, as the automatic output of the RI-optimal non-consultation running at every performance cycle.

## III. The Credentialing Register

The credentialing register is architecturally more complex than the temporal register because it does not require the outlier's ongoing presence to compound.

The professional reference network is the transmission infrastructure through which the credentialing register's contaminated account circulates. Each reference conversation that reproduces the caricature — the performance characterization that attributes limitations to the outlier's insight, effectiveness, or judgment — is both an instance of the credentialing deficit and an amplification of it. The conversation transmits the contaminated account to a new organizational context that now holds it as a prior when evaluating the outlier's subsequent contributions. The prior shapes what the new organizational actor is RI-optimal to notice: with the caricature installed as the prior, confirming evidence receives full attention weighting while disconfirming evidence is suppressed by the Precision-Weighted Epistemic Suppression mechanism before it reaches the layer where belief revision occurs.

This produces what this document names the **Credential Lemon Problem (CLP)** — an organizational instantiation of Akerlof's (1970) adverse selection mechanism in professional credentialing markets. In Akerlof's original analysis, the sellers of high-quality goods are driven from the market because buyers — unable to distinguish quality from non-quality directly — price all goods at the market average, making it unprofitable for high-quality sellers to participate. In the professional credentialing context, the receiving organization cannot distinguish accurate assessments from contaminated ones: both arrive through the standard credentialing infrastructure, both are formatted as authoritative reference conversations, both appear to represent independent multi-source confirmation. The contaminated credential circulates as a signal — in Spence's (1973) sense of an observable characteristic that the receiver uses to draw inferences about underlying quality — that has been systematically decoupled from the quality it purports to represent.

The receiving organization behaves as a rational Bayesian actor in the face of this information. It updates on the observable signal rather than on the underlying quality it cannot directly observe. The CLP is not a failure of the receiving organization's judgment. It is the predictable equilibrium behavior of an organization that has received a signal whose contamination is undetectable from the snapshot position. Stiglitz's (2000) synthesis of information economics established that information asymmetries of this kind do not self-correct through market mechanisms — they require structural interventions that change the information available to the less-informed party. The credentialing register compounds precisely because no structural intervention occurs: each receiving organization that acts on the contaminated signal adds one more institutional decision to the credentialing register's total, deepening the compound.

Rivera's (2016) sociological analysis of elite professional credentialing established that reference networks systematically favor assessments from high-status within-network sources over assessments from sources at the network's margins. The contaminated account's propagation through the professional reference network is therefore not uniform: it arrives in new organizational contexts with the authority of the network's highest-credibility sources — the same sources who built the suppression coalition and cultivated the manager relationships that constitute credibility within the network. The credentialing register's contamination arrives labeled as the most reliable information available.

## IV. Why the Two Registers Compound Together

The temporal and credentialing registers are not independent accumulation processes that happen to run concurrently. They are coupled, and the coupling is the source of the CAO's most durable property.

The temporal register's accumulated unacknowledged contributions provide the empirical content for the credentialing register's caricature. Each additional performance cycle in which the outlier's contribution goes unacknowledged without formal correction is one more episode in which the discrepancy between the actual deliverables and the formal characterization has grown — which means one more episode in which the caricature has been implicitly confirmed by institutional non-correction. Non-correction signals to every actor in the organizational network that the caricature was accurate enough to stand. Each new episode adds to the credentialing register the implicit institutional endorsement of continued non-correction.

The credentialing register's contamination in turn impedes the correction of the temporal register by reducing the probability that any future organizational context will investigate the actual performance record. A receiving organization that has received the contaminated account as its prior assigns low precision to the outlier's contributions — which means a reduced probability that those contributions will produce the visible success that would create sufficient disconfirmation pressure to motivate the actual performance record's consultation. Where visible success does occur, the contaminated prior shapes the organizational Festingerian Response: the attribution revision is more thorough precisely because the contaminated prior has pre-loaded the containing narrative before the disconfirming evidence arrives.

The formal consequence is that the combined CAO — both registers considered together — grows at a rate that exceeds the sum of the two registers' independent growth rates. The interaction term is positive: each register's growth amplifies the other's. The CAO is superadditive.

This document names the formal characterization: **The Dual-Register Accumulation Model (DRAM)** — the analytical framework describing the CAO's simultaneous growth in its temporal and credentialing registers, the positive cross-register interaction through which each register's growth amplifies the other's, and the superadditive total accumulation rate that results from the coupling. The DRAM establishes that the CAO is not two processes running in parallel. It is a coupled system whose coupling is the source of its resistance to correction: correction requires addressing both registers simultaneously, because addressing only one leaves the other intact to regenerate the corrected register's deficit within the next performance cycle. The conditions for simultaneous correction are not achievable from within the system that generated the coupling, because the system's objective function assigns negative expected utility to the consultation that would reveal each register's magnitude.

## V. The Snapshot Immunity Problem and the Epistemic Parallax

There is a specific epistemological property of the CAO that distinguishes it from ordinary institutional inaccuracy.

Ordinary institutional inaccuracy is detectable from within the current snapshot. A performance review that attributes a failure to the wrong cause is incorrect in a way that any actor with access to the performance record can identify: the record shows the actual cause, the review states something different. The inaccuracy is present as an internal inconsistency within the current documentary record.

The CAO's gap is not an internal inconsistency within the current snapshot. The current snapshot is internally consistent — every document in it coheres with every other document in it. The performance reviews cite team process. The project retrospectives attribute success to organizational methodology. The reference conversations reproduce the caricature. Nothing in the current record contradicts anything else, because the revision cycles that produced the current record also revised everything that would have produced an internal inconsistency. The cover story and the performance characterization and the project attribution are all consistent with each other because they were produced by the same revision process.

The gap is between the current snapshot and the prior states of the record that the revision cycles eliminated. It requires access to the full temporal sequence to be visible. An observer who holds only the current snapshot, however carefully and comprehensively they read it, cannot detect the CAO, because the evidence of the gap was in the documents that the revision cycles removed before the current snapshot was finalized.

This document names the structural property: **The Snapshot Immunity Problem (SIP)** — the property of the CAO that renders it undetectable by any observer who holds only the current state of the institutional record. The SIP is not an accidental feature of the CAO; it is the product of the revision cycle architecture that generates the CAO. The Institutional Self-Correcting Denial and the Organizational Confabulation Insert do not produce records that contain obvious errors. They produce records that contain accurate current facts organized into a causal framework from which the outlier's contribution was removed during the revision process. The snapshot observer reads the accurate facts and the consistent causal framework and has no basis for detecting the removal.

From the SIP follows a related property this document names **The Epistemic Parallax Effect (EPE)** — the phenomenon by which the same institutional record produces fundamentally different apparent content depending on whether the observer holds the current snapshot or the full temporal sequence. Named after optical parallax — the apparent shift in an object's position that depends on the baseline from which it is observed — the EPE names the fact that the gap is not in the document but in the distance between the document and what preceded it, and that distance is only visible from a position that holds both baselines simultaneously. From the snapshot position, the record is coherent documentation. From the temporal position, the same record is a systematic account of what the revision cycles removed. Both observations are accurate. The apparent content of the document shifts because the observational baseline shifts — not because the document changes.

The SIP and the EPE together establish the authority inversion that defines the CAO's most structurally intractable feature: the actors with institutional authority to initiate correction hold the snapshot baseline; the actor who holds the temporal baseline has no institutional authority to compel the comparison that would close the parallax.

## VI. The Path Dependence of the Institutional Record

North (1990) established path dependence as the central mechanism through which historical choices constrain present options: once an institutional path has been taken, the costs of departing from it increase with the distance already traveled, because each step along the path generates adaptations whose coherence depends on the path's continuation. The switching cost is not merely technical. It is the aggregate investment of every actor who has adapted their behavior to the existing path.

David's (1985) analysis of the QWERTY keyboard demonstrated that path-dependent structures need not be optimal to be durable — they need only to have accumulated sufficient adoption that the switching cost exceeds the improvement gain for any individual actor, even where an alternative would be superior in aggregate. The sub-optimal path persists not because no one can see the alternative but because no individual actor can afford to deviate unilaterally from the structure on which everyone else has already built.

The institutional record that the CAO inhabits is path-dependent in both senses simultaneously.

In North's sense, each document added to the contaminated record is an adaptation node. A performance review that cited team process is a node. A reference conversation that reproduced the caricature is a node. An organizational decision made on the basis of the contaminated account is a node. Each node has invested something in the path's continuation. Correction requires updating every invested node simultaneously, producing a coordination problem equivalent to the Social Commitment Ratchet's asymmetry: correction cost increases monotonically with the number of nodes, while each individual node's rational response is to maintain the committed account as long as no other node visibly revises.

In David's sense, the contaminated record need not be accurate to be durable. It needs only to have accumulated sufficient institutional endorsement — through formal documentation, through reference network saturation via the Credentialing Contagion Mechanism, through the small-world diffusion dynamics that Watts and Strogatz (1998) analyzed — that no individual organizational actor can afford to deviate from it unilaterally. The performance reviewer who might otherwise correct the record faces the prospect of revising an account that their entire reference network has endorsed. The correction cost, calculated against any single actor's available social capital, exceeds what any individual correction can absorb.

The path dependence of the institutional record produces a specific formal consequence for the CAO's durability: the CAO does not merely persist. It deepens with each new document added to the contaminated path. Each new performance review, each new reference conversation, each new organizational decision made on the basis of the contaminated account is a new node that the path's continuation now requires. The CAO grows not only through the active accumulation of new unacknowledged contributions but through the passive deepening of the path's switching cost at each step.

## VII. The Archive Asymmetry Principle

There is a structural asymmetry at the center of the CAO that no organizational process can resolve from within the system that produced it.

The outlier holds the complete temporal archive: the full sequence of states through which the institutional record passed between T₀ and Tₙ. They hold this because they were present at T₀ and at every subsequent revision cycle — the revision cycles occurred in meetings they attended, in email threads they received, in performance conversations they participated in. The full temporal sequence is available to them as memory, documentary record, and the specific experiential knowledge of watching the first clause become the second clause in real time, watching the draft that nearly acknowledged become the finalized document that did not.

Every other organizational actor holds only Tₙ: the current state of the institutional record. They hold this not through any failure of attention or intention but because the revision cycles that produced Tₙ systematically eliminated T₀ from the institutional record before it was filed. The first draft that nearly acknowledged was revised before it was sent. The performance review that nearly credited was softened through drafts before it was finalized. The ISCD ensures that the first clause survives only in the outlier's archive, not in the institutional one.

This document names the structural property: **The Archive Asymmetry Principle (AAP)** — the fundamental asymmetry between the outlier's access to the complete temporal sequence (T₀ through Tₙ) and every other organizational actor's access only to the current snapshot (Tₙ), produced and maintained by the suppression architecture's systematic elimination of prior states from institutional records. The AAP is not remediable through improved organizational documentation practices, because the process that generates the gap is the documentation process itself. The ISCD and the OCI are documentation processes — they produce formal institutional documents submitted through approved channels and signed by the appropriate authorities. The gap they generate is a product of the documentation architecture, not a failure of it. Better documentation produces more documents organized around the contaminated account; it does not restore the prior states the revision cycles removed.

The AAP has a formal consequence that Burt's (2004) structural hole analysis illuminates from an unexpected angle. The outlier's unique epistemic position — holding the complete temporal sequence that no other single actor holds — is a structural hole position in the information network: they possess non-redundant information that bridges across the temporal states of the institutional record in a way that no actor embedded within the current snapshot can replicate. In a network optimized for epistemic accuracy, this position would constitute the most valuable audit resource available. The structural hole's non-redundant information is precisely the information the gap audit requires.

The organizational architecture inverts this value. The structural hole position that generates the unique audit capacity is simultaneously the position with the least institutional authority to deploy it. The outlier who holds the complete temporal sequence cannot compel the institution to perform the comparison that would reveal what the sequence contains. Granovetter's (1973) weak-tie analysis establishes why the counter-archive's introduction into new organizational contexts through bridge ties is not a solution: the weak tie carries the accurate account as novel information, but the small-world network's saturation dynamic has already installed the contaminated account as the high-authority prior in each receiving cluster before the bridge tie's information arrives. Novel accurate information entering a cluster that has already established the contaminated account as the authoritative prior is processed at lower precision than the redundant-appearing authoritative prior — which is exactly the Precision-Weighted Epistemic Suppression mechanism operating on the correction attempt.

## VIII. The Credentialing Contagion Mechanism

The credentialing register's growth is not uniform. It propagates through specific network mechanisms that produce compounding rather than linear accumulation.

The professional reference network has three structural features that drive compounding growth in the credentialing register. First, authority concentration: the actors most likely to be contacted for reference are the actors with the highest professional credibility within the network, which in the organizational contexts the ERIE lineage documents are systematically the actors who ran the suppression coalition and built the manager relationships that constitute credibility within the network. The contaminated account arrives in new organizational contexts with the authority of the highest-credibility sources, not the median source or the outlier's own account.

Second, social contagion of non-mention: Cuc, Koppel, and Hirst's (2007) experimental documentation established that one authoritative actor's non-mention shapes subsequent recall across an entire group. When the authoritative reference source does not mention the outlier's specific contribution — reproducing the caricature without including the counterevidence — each person who hears this authoritative non-mention updates their retrieval cue structure in the direction of the authoritative source's account. The non-mention propagates as effectively as a direct assertion, and with greater naturalness: it does not require anyone to say an inaccurate thing. It requires only that everyone reproduce the accurate-appearing account that omits the counterevidence.

Third, the small-world saturation dynamic: Watts and Strogatz's (1998) topology analysis predicts rapid local cluster saturation combined with cross-cluster bridges. The contaminated account saturates the local professional cluster in a small number of transmission steps, then bridges to adjacent clusters through the weak ties that Granovetter (1973) established as the primary channels for novel professional information. Novel professional information arriving through weak ties is processed as high-value non-redundant information — which gives the contaminated account, as it enters new clusters, higher credibility than locally circulating redundant information would receive.

This document names the compound mechanism: **The Credentialing Contagion Mechanism (CCM)** — the three-factor propagation dynamic through which the credentialing register compounds through the professional reference network. The CCM produces superlinear growth in the credentialing register because the three factors amplify each other: authority concentration ensures the contaminated account arrives with maximum precision weighting; social contagion of non-mention ensures it propagates without visible distortion; small-world topology ensures it reaches new clusters ahead of any corrective account with more authority than a corrective account could carry.

DeMarzo, Vayanos, and Zwiebel's (2003) analysis of persuasion bias in social networks formalizes the compounding mechanism at the receiving-organization level: agents who receive information from multiple sources treat redundant information as independent, producing overconfidence in the direction of the correlated signal. When the professional reference network has saturated a local cluster with the contaminated account, any receiving organization that consults multiple sources within that cluster receives what appears to be independent multi-source confirmation — because the social contagion of non-mention has made the contaminated account the standard account at the cluster level. DeMarzo et al.'s persuasion bias predicts that the receiving organization over-weights the contaminated account relative to what would be warranted by its actual independence, because the account appears correlated to multiple independent sources that are in fact correlated outputs of the same suppression circuit.

## IX. The Sole Auditor

The architecture of the CAO produces a specific epistemic position that the suppression architecture did not design and cannot reach: the outlier as the sole actor who holds the complete temporal account of both registers' accumulation.

Zerubavel's (2003) analysis of collective memory established that every organized social group maintains a shared temporal narrative — an account of what the group experienced, accomplished, and decided — through the collective practice of retrieval and non-retrieval. What gets retrieved becomes more accessible; what does not get retrieved becomes less accessible; what is explicitly denied becomes actively suppressed. The organizational reference network is this collective memory practice at professional scale: the consistent retrieval of the caricature and the consistent non-retrieval of the outlier's contribution produces, through the retrieval-induced forgetting mechanism, a group memory that has forgotten the contribution not through decay but through disciplined non-mention.

But the outlier is not in the group. The outlier is the subject of the group's memory practice, not a participant in it. The outlier cannot be included in the disciplined non-mention, because the outlier was present at the original events that the collective practice is organized to forget. The outlier's memory of T₀ is not subject to the inhibitory practice trials that the reference network runs on the contribution's retrieval, because the outlier does not need the reference network to retrieve their own experience. The collective forgetting project that suppresses the contribution in every other actor's memory leaves the outlier's memory of the full temporal sequence intact.

Olick and Robbins (1998) established that the persistence of an alternative account in the face of dominant collective memory practice requires institutional support — a counter-institution that maintains the alternative account through its own retrieval practices. The outlier who holds the counter-archive does not have an institution. They have a structural hole position and the records they maintained outside the institutional archive. The counter-archive is real, accurate, documentable, and auditable. It has the epistemic content of the full temporal sequence.

What the counter-archive cannot do — and this is the asymmetry that completes the CAO's architecture — is file. There is no institutional receiving destination for the complete account. Power's (1997) sociology of audit established that audit authority derives from the institutional mandate that authorizes one party to examine another's records and issue findings the examined party is obligated to address. The outlier holds the most complete account of the CAO but has no audit mandate. The institutional actors who hold audit mandates — performance reviewers, reference network participants, promotion committee members — hold only the current snapshot and have no basis for detecting what the audit would find. The audit is possible. The auditor exists. The gap is documentable and accurate. The filing destination does not exist inside the building that generated the gap.

## X. The 2026 Synthesis

Here is the complete structure of the Compound Archive Overhang: what it contains, how it grows, and what it would take to correct it.

The temporal register accumulates each time a contribution is made, documented in the organizational systems, and not reflected in the formal institutional characterization of the person who made it. The accumulation is the gap between the email threads and the performance review, between the delivery artifacts and the reference conversation's account of the professional's capacity. The gap grows with each performance cycle in which the RI-optimal non-consultation prevents the comparison from being performed. It is self-stabilizing under the Dual-Register Accumulation Model: the larger it grows, the higher the correction cost, and the less likely any RI-optimal actor operating the status-maintenance objective function is to generate the inquiry that would initiate a correction.

The credentialing register accumulates each time the contaminated account is reproduced in a reference conversation, a performance document, or a professional network interaction. The Credentialing Contagion Mechanism propagates the contamination through the small-world reference network with authority concentration at the high-credibility nodes and persuasion bias amplification at each receiving cluster. The Credential Lemon Problem produces receiving organizations that update on the contaminated signal rather than on the underlying quality they cannot directly observe. Each organization that acts on the contaminated account adds to the credentialing register's total and deepens the path dependence of the contaminated record.

The two registers are coupled. Credentialing contamination pre-loads the Precision-Weighted Epistemic Suppression mechanism that suppresses the outlier's contributions in each new organizational context, reducing the probability of the visible success that would create sufficient disconfirmation pressure to motivate the temporal register's consultation. Temporal register accumulation provides the empirical content that the credentialing register's caricature reproduces with each new reference conversation. The Dual-Register Accumulation Model's coupling produces a superadditive total that grows at a rate exceeding either register's independent contribution.

The Snapshot Immunity Problem ensures that the current state of the institutional record, Tₙ, reveals no gap to any snapshot observer, because the revision cycles that produced Tₙ removed the evidence of the gap before finalization. The Epistemic Parallax Effect means that the same document reads as coherent documentation from the snapshot baseline and as systematic suppression from the temporal baseline — and the actors with institutional authority to initiate correction hold the snapshot baseline, while the actor who holds the temporal baseline has no institutional authority to compel the comparison that would close the parallax.

The Archive Asymmetry Principle holds the complete temporal sequence in the counter-archive position: the outlier holds T₀ through Tₙ while every organizational actor holds only Tₙ. The counter-archive cannot be reached by retrieval-induced forgetting (the outlier's memory is not subject to the reference network's inhibitory practice trials), by the Snapshot Immunity Problem (the outlier holds the temporal sequence, not only the current snapshot), or by the Social Commitment Ratchet (the outlier has no social investment in the committed account's stability). What the counter-archive cannot do is file. The record is accurate. The audit is possible. The gap is documentable with specificity in both registers. The filing destination does not exist inside the building.

Correction requires three conditions that individually cannot achieve it but together might. An external evaluator who reads the actual performance record rather than the institutional characterization — who performs the comparison that no RI-optimal internal actor will perform. A structural hole broker who can introduce the counter-archive's accurate account into the receiving network from a position of authority outside the contaminated cluster, in advance of the CCM's saturation dynamic. A blind review process that removes the source signal before evaluation, which eliminates the PWES mechanism's activation condition and allows the disconfirming evidence to reach the layer where belief revision occurs.

None of these conditions are achievable from within the system running the CAO. The correction mechanism is not blocked at the level of the evidence. The evidence is there, in the organization's own systems, in the temporal archive the outlier maintains, in the gap between the email threads and the performance reviews. The correction mechanism is blocked at the level of the institutional architecture — the filing destination for the complete account does not exist inside the architecture that generated the gap.

The record is complete. It is accurate. It cannot be filed.

The SIP creates the authority inversion that defines the CAO: actors with correction authority hold the snapshot baseline; the actor who holds the temporal baseline has no filing authority. |
| **The Epistemic Parallax Effect (EPE)** | The phenomenon by which the same institutional record produces fundamentally different apparent content depending on whether the observer holds the current snapshot baseline or the full temporal sequence baseline. Named after optical parallax — the apparent shift in an object's position as a function of the observational baseline — the EPE names the fact that the gap is not in the document but in the distance between the document and its prior states, and that distance is only measurable from a position that holds both baselines. From the snapshot position, the institutional record is coherent documentation: accurate facts organized into a consistent causal framework. From the temporal position, the same document is a record of systematic removal: the endpoint of a revision process whose prior states contained the evidence that the current state was designed to replace. Both observations are accurate descriptions of the same document; the apparent content shifts with the baseline. Distinguished from the SIP by its focus: the SIP names the structural immunity to detection from within the current record; the EPE names the position-dependent appearance of the same document that produces that immunity. |
| **The Archive Asymmetry Principle (AAP)** | The fundamental structural asymmetry from which all CAO dynamics derive: the outlier holds the complete temporal sequence (T₀ through Tₙ) while every organizational actor holds only the current snapshot (Tₙ), where this asymmetry is produced and maintained by the suppression architecture's systematic elimination of prior states from institutional records. The AAP is not remediable through improved organizational documentation practices because the process that generates the gap is the documentation process itself: the ISCD and OCI are formal documentation processes that produce the gap as their output while generating institutional records that read as accurate. The AAP has a structural hole property (Burt, 2004): the outlier's unique access to the temporal sequence is a non-redundant information position that bridges across the document's temporal states in a way no snapshot observer can replicate. The organizational architecture inverts the value of this position: the actor who holds the uniquely complete audit-relevant information holds no institutional authority to deploy it; the actors who hold institutional authority hold only the snapshot that reveals nothing to audit. The AAP is the source of the CAO's most intractable feature: the gap is auditable and the auditor exists, but the auditor holds no filing authority and the filing institution holds no complete account. |
| **The Credentialing Contagion Mechanism (CCM)** | The three-factor network propagation dynamic through which the credentialing register compounds superlinearly through the professional reference network. Factor one: authority concentration, whereby the actors most likely to be contacted for reference are systematically the actors who ran the suppression coalition and built the manager relationships that constitute professional credibility within the network — ensuring the contaminated account arrives in new organizational contexts with maximum precision weighting. Factor two: social contagion of non-mention (Cuc, Koppel & Hirst, 2007), whereby one authoritative actor's non-retrieval of the outlier's contribution shapes subsequent recall across the entire network cluster, propagating the omission as effectively as a direct assertion and without requiring any actor to state an inaccuracy. Factor three: small-world saturation dynamics (Watts & Strogatz, 1998), whereby the contaminated account saturates local professional clusters rapidly through short average path lengths, then bridges to adjacent clusters through weak ties (Granovetter, 1973) that carry it as novel high-credibility information into each new cluster before any corrective account can establish comparable authority. The CCM compounds through DeMarzo et al.'s (2003) persuasion bias: receiving organizations that consult multiple sources within a cluster saturated by the CCM receive apparent independent multi-source confirmation for the contaminated account, over-weighting it relative to what its actual dependence warrants. Distinguished from ordinary word-of-mouth reputation effects by its systematic architecture: the CCM is not random social diffusion but a structured propagation mechanism whose three factors amplify each other and whose output is consistent across organizational contexts because the suppression circuit that feeds it is consistent. |
